# Human decisions

The factory should not call the human because it became lazy. It should call the human when authority is human.

## When the human is required

Humans enter for production, mainnet, funds, secrets, budget, release, waiver, residual risk, authority change, and any action whose consequence cannot be delegated to a worker.

In those cases, the Factory prepares a decision packet. The operator decides. The factory records.

## What is not a human decision

Missing readback, attachment, worker result, consumed review, formatted proof, updated status, or internal repair is not a human decision.

That is factory obligation. Dumping it on the operator turns control into theater.

## Bad request

```text
Can I continue to production?
```

This does not say what is being approved, what evidence exists, what risk remains, or what happens if the person says no.

## Good request

```text
You are approving the onboarding v2 deploy to staging.

Includes:
- sign-up;
- email validation;
- error screen;
- loading fallback.

Does not include:
- KYC;
- payment;
- team invites;
- production.

Evidence:
- build 1842;
- onboarding tests;
- desktop/mobile screenshots;
- independent review without blocker.

Remaining risk:
abandonment analytics are not instrumented yet.

If approved:
I deploy to staging.

If refused:
I keep it blocked and open a scope repair.
```

That is a real human gate.

## What approval authorizes

Approval authorizes the declared next step, in that scope, with that evidence and risk.

It does not authorize new scope, production if the request was staging, mainnet if the request was devnet, spend if cost was not shown, or risk that was not presented.

## Record

Human decisions need a trail: who decided, when, over which artifact, with which options, consequence, and residual risk.

Chat memory is not a gate.
